# Meridian learning role-play

This is a fictional teaching exercise, not an institutional review, consent process or repository service. Meridian's projects, people and candidate archives are invented.

## Read the fictional rules before answering

The project-specific "Meridian's rules for this project" section shows the project record, named role assignments, all four catalogue practices, applicable policy requirements and the candidate archive. The task links back to it at every step. Each requirement identifies its fictional document and section, version and issuing authority: MI-DOC §2 requires a versioned change record; MI-PRES §4 calls for a manifest, format inventory, rights statement and preservation review. Restricted projects also encounter MI-PRIV §3. Discipline and community rules depend on the selected case (ASTRO-FITS §1, SOC-DDI §2, GEN-USE §5, HER-COMM agreement §2, BIO-DWC §2 or NEU-BIDS §4); the open-funder preference is FUN-OPEN §6, not an access grant. The overview reads the same catalogue as the evaluator from a byte-identical copy inside `demo/`, since Pages publishes only that directory. `tests/test_institution.py` fails if the copy drifts from `examples/meridian-institute.json`.

The role descriptions explain the exercise handoff and cite the fictional project/people assignments and policy owners; they do not appoint real officials. These short fictional rules omit actual institutional procedures, local law, contract terms, consent instruments, appeals and exceptions. A repository's listed formats, capacity and residency are only screening information. No card or reviewer response establishes lawful basis, real consent, file safety, custody, discoverability rights or permission to publish. Real reviewers must examine actual documents and material outside this simulation. The two hard holds remain hard holds.

Reviewer responses, community description/file-request questions, returned researcher corrections and curator observations now use labelled radio cards rather than dropdown answers. The separate "Acting as" selector remains for manual simulated role exploration, not answering a decision. Radio groups support Tab and arrow keys; selecting a card enables its relevant action but does not itself submit or grant authority.

## Learner route

Six cards name the discipline, researcher and situation before a choice. All 18 initial options include a concrete fictional example of a draft sentence or action, plus a gain, cost and remaining uncertainty. The selected example appears in the consequence and draft. A non-advancing option states its specific defect and the correction or outside authority needed; Retry withdraws its proposed draft without changing real files. Examples use placeholder file names and requests, never actual consent. A compact process map keeps the relevant roles and stage statuses visible together, with their questions expandable. The initial workbench view hides the draft and copy labels. The workbench generates sequential, unverified exercise-only evidence pointers for the proposed method and each correction. The learner never invents an identifier or enters subject content.

After an answer the selector moves to the owner of the next action: researcher for retry, continuation or returned work; the first reviewer after the method record; the next reviewer or curator after a review; and the reviewer or curator again after a proposed repair. On a pending outcome, hard hold or final curator observation, it preserves the answering role and explains why no next action is available in the exercise. A learner can manually switch roles to explore; the switch is explicitly a simulation and gives no authority. The pinned map and task show the currently selected role with role-specific colors, a border or outline, and the words "Current role". Keyboard focus has a separate visible outline.

The steward, privacy reviewer and, for oral history, community-appointed reviewer select a case-specific statement about the draft or missing evidence. These are training responses, not inspection, consent or permission. Description visibility and recording requests are separate decisions. A reviewer can return a named missing check; a `needs-more` response left on record also remains an open question. The researcher chooses between a case-specific correction with fictional sample wording and an inadequate shortcut; the latter leaves review blocked. The astronomy package repair likewise includes a concrete draft inventory, not an inspected list. A correction generates a new pointer and appears alongside earlier pointers in the draft, but it does not prove the underlying evidence exists. The exercise offers one return-and-repair cycle per reviewer and one curator package return, then retains unresolved checks without presenting an unrepairable loop. This is a teaching limit, not a limit on real reviews. The astronomy curator can record only that capacity, inventory and integrity remain to be checked.

Oral history stops with community and privacy decisions outstanding. Neighbourhood interviews stop with the project consent owner and privacy office needing to read actual agreements. Brain images stop with a trained image reviewer and privacy office needing to inspect actual scans and companion fields. Coastal rare-site disclosure and genomic consent remain hard holds, with no curator action. No case grants access, publishes metadata or asserts handoff readiness.

The workbench uses `textContent`, not HTML interpolation, for authored content. The state is in memory and Reset or refresh clears it. There is no upload, persistence or account. Hosts may log ordinary page requests. A generated pointer is only an exercise label; a fictional role switch does not authenticate a person or prove data safe.

## Verification

Run `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -q`, `npm test`, `npm run test:browser` and `git diff --check`. Node tests cover all six handoff routes, 18 concrete options, negative choices, evidence gating, repair and hard holds. Playwright runs each route at desktop and mobile widths, manual override, review/package returns, role markers, focus, overflow and axe-core. These checks do not establish learner comprehension or cultural appropriateness; appointed reviewers and communities must assess those separately before operational use.
