Meridian's fictional rules

These are the complete numbered sections of the ten short documents used in this exercise, not excerpts from real institutional policies. Each document carries the ID, version, authority, and cited section in the fictional source catalogue. The catalogue is the canonical list of IDs, scopes and repository screening fields. These teaching rules apply only to the six invented projects. They omit legislation, actual ethics approvals, contracts, repository terms, security controls and real consent records. No passage grants permission to disclose metadata or files, reuse human data, move custody or accept a deposit. An authorized owner must consult the real governing documents outside this exercise.

MI-DOC · Change and provenance

Version 2026.1 · fictional institutional policy board · all Meridian exercise projects.

§1 Purpose and responsibility

The researcher identifies an earlier copy, a proposed derivative and the method used to connect them. The data steward asks whether someone else could follow that account. The workbench may draft a record but cannot verify a method or inspect a file.

§2 Versioned change record

For each transformation, record the actor, date, input and output version labels, method or software version, and a pointer to quality-control evidence. Preserve the earlier account when correcting it; add a dated correction rather than silently replacing the source-to-derivative link. A generated pointer is a placeholder until a person checks its target. The steward can return an unclear link to the researcher. Documentation alone does not authorize reuse or sharing.

MI-PRES · Preservation handoff

Version 2026.1 · fictional institutional policy board · all Meridian exercise projects.

§1 Proposed package

The researcher describes a proposed package of files, versions, sizes and formats. A proposal is not a deposit. Do not send subject-level material into this teaching page.

§2 Rights and description

Record who will determine rights and proposed metadata visibility separately from file access. A private file does not make its title safe to publish. Leave both decisions open when authority or disclosure risk is unresolved.

§3 Integrity and custody

For a real handoff, the designated custodian needs a versioned manifest and file checksums, then must verify them on transfer and a repository-defined schedule. This workbench neither computes checksums nor takes custody.

§4 Curator review before acceptance

Give the curator a proposed format inventory, size estimate, rights statement, manifest plan and preservation question. The curator compares actual files and available capacity with a repository's terms, reviews integrity evidence and may return the package. A matching catalogue row is only a screening candidate; it does not prove suitability, acceptance, preservation or permission to release.

MI-PRIV · Confidentiality review

Version 2026.2 · fictional institutional policy board · restricted exercise projects.

§1 Trigger

Restricted classification prompts a privacy review. The label alone does not establish a lawful basis, valid consent or a safe description. The researcher identifies the intended purpose without entering personal content into this exercise.

§2 Separate questions

The privacy reviewer asks who may see the descriptive metadata and who may request the underlying files. They also ask whether a proposed derivative can expose people through combination with other information. An archive's mediated-access capability is not an approval.

§3 Review and unresolved authority

Record the requested purpose, proposed controls, consent or confidentiality limits and missing evidence for an authorized privacy reviewer. Return an inadequate plan for revision; keep any unresolved question open. The reviewer cannot infer owner consent, approve a new use merely from a draft, or override an independent community or location-risk decision. Actual disclosure and access require the applicable external authority.

ASTRO-FITS · Astronomy method record

Version 1.0 · fictional astronomy panel · astronomy exercise project.

§1 Calibration lineage

Record the FITS processing history, input sky image version, calibration or workflow identifier and output version. Link a proposed quality check to the run so a steward can ask how the large derivative was made. The Large-Object Archive's FITS/Zarr and scale fields are candidate screening facts, not evidence that the run or transfer took place.

SOC-DDI · Interview description

Version 1.0 · fictional social science panel · social-science exercise project.

§1 Collection context

The researcher identifies the questionnaire or interview guide and a versioned coding method without copying interview text into the workbench. The steward asks which material was transformed and which description may remain private.

§2 Variables and use limits

Prepare a codebook and DDI-compatible variable metadata with definitions for coded fields. Check titles, categories and small groups for disclosure before any metadata export. A codebook makes data understandable; it is not participant consent or a release decision. The privacy reviewer must assess the proposed purpose and confidentiality limits separately.

GEN-USE · Genomic reuse

Version 2.0 · fictional genomics panel · genomics exercise project.

§1 Proposed purpose

The researcher describes the intended analysis of a derivative without submitting sequence or variant content to this page. A purpose statement is a proposal, not a finding that reuse is allowed.

§2 Source and derivative

Keep source and variant-call version labels, workflow version and an unverified quality-control pointer in the change record. The steward can ask for a clearer connection between them.

§3 Storage candidate

The Secure Data Vault is a candidate for restricted NL material and may screen by format, size and mediated or enclave access. Its listing does not supply a consent decision or approve a genomic use.

§4 Review material

Send the proposed purpose and available consent restrictions to the authorized consent owner and privacy reviewer outside the exercise. If the scope of consent is unknown, record that gap rather than selecting an assumed permission.

§5 Reuse hold

Do not advance a new use while consent scope and approved purpose remain unknown. Only the authorized consent owner can determine whether the use is covered, with privacy review where required. A training response or revised wording cannot clear this hold, and repository capability is not a substitute for owner authorization.

HER-COMM agreement · Community-governed collection

Version 1.0 · fictional community-appointed circle · community-governed exercise project. This is an invented agreement, not a representation of any real community's protocol.

§1 Authority

The community-appointed circle holds the decision about proposed uses and discoverability under this fictional agreement. The researcher and steward can prepare a question, but cannot speak for the circle. Institutional privacy review remains a separate obligation.

§2 Description and recording requests

Ask the circle separately whether even a title or catalogue description may be visible and whether requests for recordings may be considered. Start with private metadata and no presumed request route. Record the circle's decision outside this exercise before changing either setting. A practice-role answer does not grant access, waive an agreement or transfer community authority to a repository.

BIO-DWC · Occurrence locations

Version 1.0 · fictional ecology panel · biodiversity exercise project.

§1 Observation record

Keep the source observation version and the method used to produce generalized coordinates. Prepare Darwin Core-compatible occurrence metadata with the coordinate precision explained. Do not place exact rare-site coordinates in a public title, preview or teaching input.

§2 Sensitive location review

A generalized grid is a proposed mitigation, not proof that the site cannot be inferred. Keep location-bearing metadata private until an independent ecology or location-risk authority reviews disclosure and residual re-identification risk outside this exercise. The researcher, privacy reviewer, curator and open-funder preference cannot clear that hold by choosing broader cells here.

NEU-BIDS · Imaging description

Version 1.0 · fictional imaging panel · neuroimaging exercise project.

§1 Input and proposed derivative

Identify the imaging session version and proposed derivative version without uploading a scan. State which processing method is proposed.

§2 Method evidence

Keep the defacing pipeline version and a pointer to a proposed check. A pointer generated by this page is unverified and cannot show that defacing succeeded.

§3 Access separation

Private descriptive metadata, mediated access to files and a repository's technical capability are distinct. Confirmed consent as a project label does not resolve each planned use or disclosure on its own.

§4 Sidecars and de-identification

Prepare BIDS-compatible sidecars and an inventory of potentially identifying image and JSON fields for a human de-identification review. The privacy reviewer must examine the actual material and use limits outside the workbench. Neither the draft nor a proposed vault package certifies BIDS conformance or safe release.

FUN-OPEN · Open outputs preference

Version 1.0 · fictional funder officer · open-futures exercise projects.

§1 Aim

The funder prefers reusable descriptions and, where suitable, open derivatives. This is an exercise preference rather than a grant of rights.

§2 Data and description

Evaluate a metadata record separately from file access. A public title may reveal a location or person even when the underlying file stays private.

§3 Rights

The researcher records proposed rights and restrictions; an authorized owner checks them. Do not assume that collecting or processing material gives the researcher permission to publish it.

§4 Suitable derivative

Describe how an open derivative might remove restricted content while preserving a useful method account. The proposed transformation needs actual review before publication.

§5 Conflicting limits

Consent, confidentiality, community agreement and sensitive-site review take precedence over this preference in this exercise. Document why an output remains private or under review instead of treating openness as a deadline override.

§6 Conditional publication preference

Publish reusable metadata or a suitable open derivative only after the relevant rights and disclosure authorities have approved the specific output. If an independent ecology review has not resolved rare-site risk, retain private metadata and do not open the location derivative. The funder preference does not grant access or clear any hold.

Candidate repository screening, not acceptance

The catalogue records four invented services. The General Archive screens CSV, JSON, TXT, TIFF, PDFA and Parquet up to 500 GiB for open-candidate or restricted material in NL/EU, with public or private metadata and open or mediated access. The Secure Data Vault screens CSV, JSON, TXT, WAV, FASTQ, VCF, NII and Parquet up to 50,000 GiB for restricted material in NL, private metadata and mediated or enclave access. The Large-Object Archive screens FITS, HDF5, Zarr, Parquet and CSV up to 200,000 GiB for open-candidate material in NL/EU with public metadata and open access. The Community-Governed Collection screens WAV, TIFF, TXT, JSON and CSV up to 2,000 GiB for community-governed restricted material in NL with private metadata and mediated access. All four are candidate-only; the curator must inspect actual files, terms, rights, capacity and integrity before any real acceptance. The catalogue entries are canonical for exact capability fields.
